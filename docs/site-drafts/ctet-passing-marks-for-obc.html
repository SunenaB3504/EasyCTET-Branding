CTET Passing Marks for OBC, SC, ST & General Category (2026 Rules)

The Core Fact: CBSE's official CTET qualifying benchmark is 60% (90 marks out of 150) for all candidates. However, under NCTE guidelines, recruiting authorities (KVS, DSSSB, State Governments) apply a 55% relaxation (82.5 marks, accepted as 82) for reserved categories according to their own recruitment rules.
⚠️ Common Misunderstanding: CBSE does not issue a "Pass/Fail" verdict at 82 marks on its marksheet; it issues marks and eligibility statements. Whether your 82 marks makes you eligible for a teaching post depends entirely on the specific appointing authority and whether your OBC status is recognized on their central or state list.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does CBSE officially declare 82 marks as pass for OBC in CTET?

No. Under Clause 9 of the CTET Information Bulletin, CBSE sets a uniform qualifying benchmark of 60% (90 out of 150 marks). However, CBSE explicitly notes that appointing authorities may grant concessions. In practice, recruiting bodies (KVS, DSSSB, State Governments) apply a 55% qualification standard (82.5 marks, rounded to 82) for reserved categories according to their own rules.

Can an OBC candidate from another state use 82 marks in Delhi DSSSB?

No. Delhi Subordinate Services Selection Board (DSSSB) strictly requires a Delhi-issued OBC certificate to claim category relaxation. If you belong to the OBC category in UP, Bihar, Rajasthan, or any other state, DSSSB treats you as an Unreserved/General applicant, meaning you must score at least 90 marks in CTET.

What are the CTET passing marks for BPSC TRE in Bihar?

Under Bihar Education Department recruitment guidelines, General Male candidates require 90 marks (60%). Backward Classes (BC), Extremely Backward Classes (EBC), and General category Female candidates are eligible at 82 marks (55%). SC, ST, and PwD candidates require 75 marks (50%).

Is CTET mandatory for in-service teachers?

Yes. Following Supreme Court directives reinforcing Section 23 of the Right to Education (RTE) Act, appointed teachers who do not hold a recognized TET qualification must clear CTET or their respective State TET to meet statutory service standards.
