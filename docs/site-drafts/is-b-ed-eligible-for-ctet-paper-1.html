Is B.Ed Eligible for CTET Paper 1? Supreme Court Ruling Explained

By EasyCTET Legal & Research Team · Fact-checked against official Supreme Court and NCTE rulings · Last updated: 3 October 2026

Short answer: No. B.Ed degree holders are legally ineligible to appear for CTET Paper 1 (Primary: Classes I–V) and cannot be appointed as Primary School Teachers (PRT) anywhere in India.

On August 11, 2023, the Supreme Court of India in Devesh Sharma v. Union of India quashed the NCTE notification of 2018 that previously permitted B.Ed graduates into primary schools. Primary teaching requires specialized Diploma in Elementary Education (D.El.Ed) training.

Why the Supreme Court Disqualified B.Ed from Paper 1

The landmark Supreme Court judgment (Civil Appeal No. 5068 of 2023) established that the Right to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act guarantees not merely schooling, but quality meaningful education by pedagogically trained educators:

Early Childhood vs. Secondary Pedagogy: The curriculum of a 2-year D.El.Ed (Diploma in Elementary Education) is calibrated exclusively for young learners aged 6–11, focusing on child cognitive development, foundational literacy, and primary pedagogical methods. Conversely, Bachelor of Education (B.Ed) degrees train candidates for secondary and higher secondary teaching (ages 11–16).

The Court observed that attempting to bridge this gap with an arbitrary 6-month post-appointment bridge course diluted statutory elementary teaching standards.

What About Candidates Who Passed Paper 1 Before August 2023?

In subsequent clarification orders (April 2024), the Supreme Court clarified the retrospective boundary of the decision:

  • Protected In-Service Appointments: Teachers holding B.Ed degrees who were appointed to primary schools prior to August 11, 2023, under recruitment processes that were not subject to pending judicial challenges, remain protected.
  • Ongoing and Future Selections: For all subsequent teacher recruitments across Central schools (KVS, NVS), state departments (such as Bihar BPSC TRE and DSSSB), and CTET exam cycles, B.Ed holders are excluded from Primary Teacher eligibility.
  • CBSE Policy: The Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) aligned CTET information bulletins with this judgment, withholding Paper 1 eligibility certificates from candidates registering solely with a B.Ed qualification.

What Should B.Ed Candidates Focus on Now?

Rather than disputing primary eligibility, B.Ed degree holders should channel preparation toward examinations where their qualification is fully valid and in high demand:

  1. CTET Paper 2 (Upper Primary VI–VIII): Prepare systematically for either the Mathematics & Science stream or the Social Studies / Social Science stream (60 marks each). Paper 2 certification is permanent and valid nationwide for life.
  2. State Teacher Eligibility Tests: CTET Paper 2 qualifies candidates directly for Upper Primary recruitment in states such as Uttar Pradesh and Kerala (Category 2). Compare cross-state requirements in our All-India TET Carry-Over Guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can B.Ed candidates appear for CTET Paper 1 just for practice?

While CBSE's registration portal may physically accept an application if false qualifications are entered, CBSE does not issue qualification certificates to candidates declaring only a B.Ed. Furthermore, any certificate obtained through misrepresentation is legally void ab initio under the Supreme Court order and will be summarily rejected during employer document verification.

Can D.El.Ed candidates apply for CTET Paper 2?

Yes. Any candidate holding a 2-year Diploma in Elementary Education (D.El.Ed, BTC, or JBT) who has also completed a Bachelor's Degree (such as BA, B.Sc, or B.Com) is fully eligible to sit CTET Paper 2 for classes VI to VIII.

Are 1st year B.Ed students eligible to appear for CTET Paper 2?

Yes. In an official clarification dated August 4, 2022, the National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE) affirmed that any student enrolled in an approved teacher training course is considered "pursuing" from their date of admission and is legally entitled to sit TET exams.

Can B.Ed holders teach in private primary schools?

Under Section 23(1) of the RTE Act and CBSE Affiliation Bye-Laws, all affiliated private unaided schools must adhere to NCTE minimum qualification norms. Private schools cannot appoint B.Ed holders to primary posts without risking affiliation penalties.
