Central Teacher Eligibility Test (CTET) 2026: Blueprint, Rules & Syllabus Guide

By EasyCTET Research Team · Fact-checked against official CBSE and NCTE bulletins · Last updated: 3 October 2026

The Central Teacher Eligibility Test (CTET) is India's national benchmark for school teaching eligibility, conducted twice each year by the Central Board of Secondary Education (CBSE) under guidelines established by the National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE).

Qualifying CTET is mandatory for appointment in Central Government schools (Kendriya Vidyalayas, Navodaya Vidyalayas, Central Tibetan Schools) and schools under Union Territories (including Chandigarh, Delhi, and Andaman & Nicobar). It is also accepted by numerous state governments in lieu of their state TET.

Critical Legal & Regulatory Precedents

1. Primary Eligibility (B.Ed vs D.El.Ed): Following the Supreme Court judgment in Devesh Sharma v. Union of India (August 2023), B.Ed qualification is not recognized for Primary teaching (Classes I–V). Candidates must hold a 2-year Diploma in Elementary Education (D.El.Ed, B.El.Ed, or equivalent) to qualify for Paper 1. Read the full B.Ed Supreme Court verdict analysis →

2. Lifetime Certificate Validity: In accordance with NCTE Circular No. 76-4/2010/NCTE/Acad dated 09.06.2021, CTET qualifying certificates possess lifetime validity across all appointments. Qualifying once fulfills lifelong eligibility.

3. In-Service Teacher Compliance: In September 2025, the Supreme Court upheld the mandatory requirement of teacher eligibility certification for all in-service teachers, reinforcing that statutory teacher standards cannot be relaxed retrospectively.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is CTET mandatory for private school teachers?

Under Section 23(1) of the RTE Act and CBSE Affiliation Bye-Laws, all CBSE-affiliated schools (including private unaided schools) are mandated to appoint only TET/CTET qualified teachers for primary and middle school classes.

Can a candidate appear for both Paper 1 and Paper 2?

Yes. Candidates meeting educational qualifications for both primary (Classes I–V) and upper primary (Classes VI–VIII) levels may register for and sit both papers in the same examination session.

What is the difference between Language I and Language II?

Language I tests proficiency in the medium of instruction with higher emphasis on literary comprehension and pedagogical framing. Language II tests communication, reading, and language development skills. Candidates cannot select the same language for both Language I and II.
