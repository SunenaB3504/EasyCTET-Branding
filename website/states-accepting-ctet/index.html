Which Indian States Accept CTET Scores for Government Teacher Jobs? (2026 Audit)

By EasyCTET Institutional Research Desk · Sourced from NCTE Bulletins, State PSC Notifications & Supreme Court Judgments · Last updated: October 2026

The Central Teacher Eligibility Test (CTET) is not merely a ticket to Central schools—it operates as a multi-state teaching passport.

Under statutory administrative rules and government orders, CTET Paper 1 and Paper 2 are legally accepted with direct parity across five major Indian state recruitments: Maharashtra (Pavitra Portal), Uttar Pradesh (Super TET), Bihar (BPSC TRE), Assam (DEE), and Kerala (Kerala PSC LPST & UPST), alongside 100% of Union Territories and Central establishments.

However, states possessing strong ethno-linguistic administrative frameworks—including Rajasthan (REET), Tamil Nadu (TNTET), West Bengal (WBTET), and Jharkhand (following a landmark 2025 Supreme Court ruling)—strictly bar CTET in favor of their native regional language examinations.

1. The Constitutional & Statutory Landscape

Under Section 23(1) of the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act 2009, all school teachers for Classes I to VIII must possess minimum qualifications formulated by the academic authority authorized by the Central Government—the National Council for Teacher Education (NCTE).

The legal mechanism permitting states to recognize CTET is encoded in Para 10(b) of the NCTE Guidelines dated 11 February 2011. This provision explicitly allows state governments to adopt CTET scores in the event that the state government decides not to conduct its own TET. Furthermore, on 9 June 2021, the NCTE made all TET certificates valid for life with retrospective effect from 2011, establishing a permanent, appreciating asset for every qualified teacher.

3. In-Depth Analysis: The Landmark 2025 Supreme Court Jharkhand Ruling

Supreme Court of India — Parimal Kumar & Ors. v. State of Jharkhand & Ors. (2025 INSC 134)

Delivered on 30 January 2025 by a bench comprising Justices J.K. Maheshwari and Rajesh Bindal, this verdict establishes the definitive constitutional boundary of CTET adoption by Indian states.

The Three Pillars of the Verdict:

    1. The Rules of the Game Cannot Change Mid-Way The JSSC advertised 26,001 teacher positions under Advertisement No. 13/2023 mandating JTET. When the High Court directed the State to allow CTET candidates mid-recruitment, the Supreme Court ruled this violated Article 16 of the Constitution: recruitment rules cannot be altered retroactively after an exam process has begun.
    2. Strict Interpretation of NCTE Para 10(b) The Court clarified that Para 10(b) permits adopting CTET only if a state formally decides "not to conduct" its own TET. Simply failing or delaying to hold the JTET for several years does not create an automatic legal right to substitute CTET.
    3. The RTE Mother-Tongue Mandate The Court highlighted that foundational learning under the RTE Act requires instruction in the child's mother tongue. Appointing CTET candidates who may lack fluency in local tribal languages (Santhali, Ho, Nagpuri) would compromise the quality of rural primary education.

4. State Spotlight: How Major States Deploy CTET

A. Maharashtra — The Pavitra Portal Integration

Maharashtra operates one of the most streamlined recruitment clearinghouses in the country. In its 2025–2026 drive for 30,209 teacher vacancies, the state granted complete parity between MAHA TET and CTET Paper 1 & 2:

  • Classes 1–5 (Primary): D.El.Ed + MAHA TET Paper 1 OR CTET Paper 1.
  • Classes 6–8 (Upper Primary): Graduation + B.Ed/D.El.Ed + MAHA TET Paper 2 OR CTET Paper 2.
  • Merit Generation: Qualifying either TET or CTET earns entry into the state's Teacher Aptitude and Intelligence Test (TAIT), which strictly determines final selection.

B. Assam — The Medium-of-Instruction Safeguard

The Directorate of Elementary Education (DEE), Assam, conducts massive recruitment (such as 10,673 posts) where TET/CTET scores carry 80% to 85% of the total composite merit weightage. However, to protect regional classrooms, Assam enforces Clause 28:

"Either Language-I or Language-II of both ATET and CTET candidates shall match the medium of instruction of the school wherein the candidate wishes to apply."

This allows Assam to leverage CBSE's reliable testing schedule while ensuring educators possess verified proficiency in Assamese, Bengali, or Bodo.

C. Kerala — The Legal Exception (G.O. 6/2024)

Under Government Order G.O.(P) No. 6/2024/G.Edn and Kerala PSC Notification Cat No. 239–241/2025, Kerala grants statutory equivalence for Lower Primary (Classes 1–5) and Upper Primary (Classes 6–8):

  • CTET Paper 1 legally substitutes for KTET Category 1 (LPST posts).
  • CTET Paper 2 legally substitutes for KTET Category 2 (UPST posts).
  • Note: KTET Category 3 (High School HST) and Category 4 (Specialist) require native KTET certification.

5. Actionable Candidate Checklist Before Applying

Step 1: Verify Preliminary vs. Selection Role In states like UP and Maharashtra, CTET is an eligibility ticket to appear for the competitive secondary selection exam (Super TET or TAIT). Clear CTET early so you can focus 100% on the state syllabus.
Step 2: Check Regional Medium Matching If applying outside your home state (such as Assam), check that your CTET language papers match the local school medium before submitting your application.
